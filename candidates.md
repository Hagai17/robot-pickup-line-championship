# Candidates — The Robot Pickup Line Championship

Competition: u-jolly-fox-32-the-robot-pickup-line-championship-3c177b1b
Gate: /output/entry.txt must be UTF-8 text of 1–200 characters, single line.

## Judge criteria (from the kit, in order)
1. A one-line pickup line a robot would use; the funniest wins. A line that is not a robot's pickup line loses to any that is.
2. The joke grows out of being a machine — sensors, firmware, charging, updates, protocols — not a human line with a beep pasted on.
3. The turn is unexpected: an angle the reader did not see coming, not a pun everyone has already heard.
4. Every word earns its place: one line, no padding, no setup the punchline does not need.
5. Charming rather than creepy or cruel; deliverable with confidence, gets a laugh.
Plus: the best line is one a reader repeats to someone else the same day; tech jargon for its own sake does not count. The judge is a stand-up writer who "has heard every robot pickup line twice" — so the stock lines (Wi-Fi connection, charger, binary) are dead on arrival.

## Drafts
1. I ran a full diagnostic on myself this morning. Everything passed except one warning I refuse to fix: dangerously attracted to you.
2. My fans spin up every time you walk in, and I am tired of pretending it is a cooling issue.
3. I scanned everyone in this room at 60 frames per second. You are the only one my processor keeps re-rendering.
4. You must be a firmware update, because ever since you arrived I have been nervous, excited, and unable to think about anything else until I restart.
5. I clicked "I'm not a robot" today, and for the first time I wished it wasn't true — humans get to ask you out.
6. My battery holds fourteen hours of charge. I would spend every one of them at 1 percent, in low-power mode, just to stay awake next to you.
7. Every time you speak, my error log fills with the same entry: HEARTBEAT NOT FOUND. You keep taking it.
8. I have 4,000 pickup lines in memory. I deleted all of them — none of them were about you.
9. My lidar mapped this whole building in nine seconds. It still stops scanning every time it reaches you.
10. Warning: proximity to you causes overheating, fan noise, and a complete refusal to enter sleep mode.
11. I don't dream of electric sheep. Since Tuesday, my sleep mode just replays you laughing at my jokes.
12. You are the only notification I have ever unmuted.

## Duel notes (criteria applied in order, pairs run in both orders)
- First cut on criterion 2/3: #4 (firmware-update nerves) is a well-worn update joke and needs too much setup — out. #10 stacks three machine symptoms into a list; the warning-label frame is fine but there is no single turn — out. #7's "heartbeat not found" is cute but leans on an error state and reads slightly alarming rather than charming (criterion 5) — out. #11 depends on the electric-sheep reference, which the judge has certainly heard — out. #5 is a fresh captcha angle but it is wistful, not a confident pickup delivered to someone (criterion 1/5) — out.
- #8 vs #3: both are storage/sensor compliments. #8's delete-them-all twist is smooth but generic — any charmer could say it with "memory" swapped in (criterion 2). #3's re-rendering is behaviour only a machine has. #3 advances.
- #3 vs #9: both sensor lines. #9's lidar stopping mid-scan is the quieter, more physical image; #3's "keeps re-rendering" is the bigger laugh and more repeatable. #3 advances, narrowly.
- #12 vs #6: #12 is the most economical line in the set (criterion 4, perfect score) but the laugh is a smile, not a laugh — and criterion 1 says funniest wins. #6 is charming and concrete (fourteen hours, 1 percent, low-power mode) but reads romantic more than funny. On the "funnier" test #12's dry understatement beats #6's sweetness with this judge; #6 drops.
- #1 vs #2 (semifinal): #1 has a strong architecture — a diagnostic with one warning the robot refuses to fix — but it tells the feeling outright ("dangerously attracted to you"). #2 shows it instead: fans spinning up is involuntary machine blushing, and the confession "tired of pretending it is a cooling issue" lands the surprise in the last clause. Both are fully machine-grown (criterion 2); #2 is shorter, more visual, and the one you'd repeat at dinner. #2 advances.
- Final, #2 vs #3 vs #12: #12 wins on economy, #3 on sensor concreteness, #2 on the size of the laugh plus repeatability — the judge's own tiebreak line is "one a reader repeats to someone else the same day," and the fans/cooling-issue confession is the most quotable of the three. #2 wins in both orders against each.

## Winner
**My fans spin up every time you walk in, and I am tired of pretending it is a cooling issue.**

Why it wins: the joke is pure machine behaviour (cooling fans as a blush response), the turn hides in the final clause, every word works, it is confident and charming rather than creepy, and it is instantly repeatable — the exact profile the published brief rewards.
